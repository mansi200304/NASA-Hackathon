# Validation against ADWR

How our Sentinel-1 rates compare with the Arizona Department of Water Resources' own published InSAR products. Written for the submission; every number is generated by `src/validate_adwr.py` into `data/processed/adwr_validation.json`.

## What ADWR publishes (data used)

From the ADWR GIS Data and Maps page (https://www.azwater.gov/gis, "Arizona Active Land Subsidence", updated Sept 2026):

- **Subsidence-area polygons**, May 2026: `updatedlandsubsidenceshapefile.zip` (32 polygons, 22 named features).
- **Sentinel-1 deformation raster**, 2017-02 to 2026-05: `AZdeformation_2017to2026.zip`. Same satellite family as our data, processed separately by ADWR.
- **RADARSAT-2 deformation raster**, 2010-05 to 2026-05: `AZDeformation_2010to2026.zip`. An independent sensor over a longer period.

Files are downloaded to `data/raw/adwr/` (not committed) from `https://www.azwater.gov/sites/default/files/zip/<name>.zip`.

## Units are inferred, not documented

ADWR does not state the raster units in the files or on its pages. We regressed ADWR's value against our own cumulative line-of-sight displacement at the same 11,685 sinking pixels (see the script): slope **0.0093** (about 0.01), correlation 0.98, so the values are **metres**. We treat them as line-of-sight, because they match ours closely without a vertical factor. Anywhere units matter, say "inferred".

## Result 1: rates over the same pixels (our sinking pixels, 1 cm/yr or faster)

| Area | Pixels | Ours (cm/yr) | ADWR Sentinel-1 | Difference | Correlation | ADWR RADARSAT-2 | Correlation | ADWR feature that holds our pixels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eloy - Picacho | 945 | -1.23 | -1.57 | +0.34 | 0.59 | -1.09 | 0.18 | Picacho 100% |
| Willcox basin | 4,292 | -2.38 | -2.39 | +0.01 | 0.98 | -2.31 | 0.97 | Fort Grant Rd 12%, Kansas Settlement 88% |
| Fort Grant Road (north of Willcox) | 1,292 | -4.66 | -4.61 | -0.05 | 0.99 | -4.24 | 0.98 | Fort Grant Rd 100% |
| Elfrida area | 1,415 | -2.86 | -2.83 | -0.03 | 0.98 | -3.31 | 0.97 | Elfrida 100% |
| Bowie - San Simon | 3,741 | -2.17 | -2.28 | +0.11 | 0.99 | -2.30 | 0.94 | Bowie-San Simon 100% |

Medians in cm/yr along the line of sight; negative is sinking. Correlations are pixel by pixel.

- In the four southeast areas our median is within **0.01 to 0.11 cm/yr** of ADWR's Sentinel-1 value, and the correlation is **0.98 to 0.99**. Against the independent RADARSAT-2 record the medians differ by 0.07 to 0.45 cm/yr with correlation **0.94 to 0.98**.
- At **Eloy - Picacho** the agreement is weak: our median is 0.34 cm/yr less negative than ADWR's Sentinel-1, the pixel correlation is only 0.59, and with RADARSAT-2 it is 0.18. Eloy is **not validated** by this test.
- The Sentinel-1 comparison is a **consistency check**, not independent: same satellite family, different processing. RADARSAT-2 is the independent evidence.

## Result 2: unbiased comparison over every valid pixel

The table above only uses pixels where we already see sinking. Over **every** valid pixel of each frame:

| Frame | Pixels | Correlation with ADWR Sentinel-1 | Typical difference (median absolute) | Correlation with RADARSAT-2 | RADARSAT-2 correlation on our sinking pixels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Southeast (Willcox area) | 27,774 | 0.991 | 0.16 cm/yr | 0.977 | 0.958 |
| Central (Eloy, Phoenix valley) | 47,258 | 0.714 | 0.18 cm/yr | 0.802 | 0.002 |

In the southeast the two records agree almost everywhere. In the central frame, agreement is lower overall and **near zero on the pixels we flag as sinking** (RADARSAT-2). That is the Eloy result again: the signal there is small (about 1 to 2 cm/yr) and sits close to the noise.

## Result 3: do our sinking areas fall inside ADWR's official features?

- **95.4%** of our sinking pixels in the southeast frame, and **82.7%** in the central frame, lie inside an official ADWR subsidence polygon.
- **100%** of each study area's sinking pixels lie inside one matching feature: Eloy-Picacho in **Picacho**, the Willcox basin in **Kansas Settlement** (88%) and **Fort Grant Rd** (12%), the Fort Grant Road area in **Fort Grant Rd**, the Elfrida area in **Elfrida**, Bowie-San Simon in **Bowie-San Simon**. Study-area names use ADWR's names.
- The reverse is not true: we flag **40.4%** of ADWR's southeast feature area and only **2.6%** of its central feature area. ADWR's polygons are broad outlines of areas that have moved since 2010, many now sinking slowly (for example the West Valley feature: ours -0.07, ADWR's Sentinel-1 -0.25 cm/yr). This agrees with our finding that the West Valley shows no sinking above 1 cm/yr.

## A measured reference offset

Over ADWR features where little is happening, ADWR's Sentinel-1 medians are **0.2 to 0.4 cm/yr more negative** than ours (for example Harquahala Valley: ours +0.06, ADWR -0.30; Avra Valley: ours +0.17, ADWR -0.23). That is a difference in reference choice (we set each frame's median ground to zero). It is the bias the Limits section warns about, now measured. We include it in the uncertainty published for each study area.

## Mines

ADWR's Sentinel-1 raster has no data within 3 km of any of the four mine points we excluded (Sierrita, Mission, Ray, Morenci), and no ADWR feature lies within 1.5 km of them. This is consistent with our reading that those are mining signals, not mapped groundwater subsidence, but it does not independently prove it.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows our Sentinel-1 processing reproduces ADWR's published rates and patterns in the southeast, with the independent RADARSAT-2 record agreeing.
- It does **not** validate Eloy, and it does **not** validate NISAR at all (NISAR is too new for ADWR's products).
- Both are line-of-sight values referenced to a frame median; neither is vertical subsidence.
