UNDERSTANDING A LANDSCAPE IN MOTION

Arizona is sinking.The evidence is beneath the surface.

When groundwater is withdrawn, underground sediments can compact and the land above can move. Satellites help reveal that movement—even when it is too slow to see on the ground.

Sinkronized brings satellite radar observations, Arizona water-agency comparisons and uncertainty into one place. Explore what we can measure, where the evidence is still limited, and why that matters.

Why two radar viewing directions matter +

Satellites make ascending (generally south-to-north) and descending (generally north-to-south) passes. Because the radar looks obliquely at the ground, each pass measures movement in a different direction. Comparing the two can strengthen confidence and help separate motion components, but it still requires careful reference and geometry checks.

Radar reports movement along its viewing direction; an observed rate is not automatically a vertical sinking rate or proof of its cause.

THE SCIENCE / DOCUMENTED EVIDENCE

Why does Arizona's ground sink?

Groundwater withdrawal is an established cause of land subsidence in Arizona's sediment-filled basins. Agricultural irrigation is a major demand for groundwater in several of those basins. The evidence comes from long-term well records, water-use reports, land-cover studies and satellite observations—not from a satellite image alone.

Agricultural water use in the Willcox Basin, 2022.

03 / PEER-REVIEWED RESEARCH
+29% agricultural land cover

Growing irrigation demand is linked to falling groundwater levels.

Job and colleagues found a 29% increase in Willcox Basin agricultural land cover from 2008–2021 and a strong association between agriculture and groundwater-level decline. This supports a basin-wide explanation, not attribution at an individual satellite pixel.

Job et al. · Geosciences (2023) ↗

What is established: excessive groundwater extraction can cause subsidence, and agricultural irrigation is a major groundwater use in the Willcox Basin. What remains to be tested here: how much each local motion signal is attributable to pumping rather than other environmental or measurement effects.

Arizona-wide research also shows that similar pumping volumes can be associated with different subsidence responses across basins. Groundwater use, geology, measurement quality and time period all matter.

Majumdar et al. · Hydrological Processes (2022) ↗
